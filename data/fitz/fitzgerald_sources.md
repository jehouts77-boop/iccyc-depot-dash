# Edmund Fitzgerald remembrance - words and sources

The text shown by `presto/screens/fitz.py` (kept in `presto/fitz_data.py`).
Every statement was checked against the sources below on 2026-10-07 and
approved by Jason the same day. Same standard as On This Day: straight facts,
each one sourced. To change the wording, change it here first, then in
`fitz_data.py`.

Sources
- W = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Edmund_Fitzgerald
- M = Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum, https://www.shipwreckmuseum.com/edmund-fitzgerald/
  and https://www.shipwreckmuseum.com/edmund-fitzgerald/the-fateful-journey/
- S = https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wreck_of_the_Edmund_Fitzgerald (the song: facts only, no lyrics)
- Crew list: https://www.ssefo.com/crew/index.html, the Mesabi Tribune
  (https://www2.mesabitribune.com/mine/remembering-those-who-lost-their-lives-on-the-fitzgerald/article_65a6553f-d9c7-4b22-99d3-78cc92f6ca8a.html)
  and NetNewsLedger (https://netnewsledger.com/?p=157642) - the three lists agree on
  all 29 names, positions, ages and home towns.

| title | THE EDMUND FITZGERALD / 1958 - 1975 | W |
| 1 | Launched in June 1958 at River Rouge, Michigan, she was 729 feet long: the largest ship on the Great Lakes. | W |
| 2 | She belonged to the Northwestern Mutual Life Insurance Company and was named for its chairman. | W, M |
| 3 | For 17 years she carried taconite, pellets of iron ore, from the Minnesota iron range to the mills of Detroit and Toledo. Six times she set the record for a season's haul. | W |
| 4 | On the afternoon of November 9, 1975, she left Superior, Wisconsin, with 26,116 tons of taconite, bound for a steel mill near Detroit. Captain Ernest M. McSorley commanded a crew of 28. | W, M |
| 5 | The next day a storm crossed Lake Superior with near-hurricane winds. The freighter Arthur M. Anderson followed ten miles astern. | W, M |
| 6 | At 3:30 p.m. McSorley radioed: 'I have a fence rail down, two vents lost or damaged, and a list.' Later he reported both radars gone. | M, W |
| 7 | By evening the Anderson logged winds of 58 knots and seas of 18 to 25 feet. | M |
| 8 | At 7:10 p.m. McSorley told the Anderson: 'We are holding our own.' Minutes later the Fitzgerald was gone from the radar. No distress signal was received. | M, W |
| 9 | She lies in two pieces in 530 feet of water, about 17 miles from Whitefish Bay. All 29 men aboard were lost. None was ever found. | W |
| 10 | Why she sank has never been settled. The Coast Guard and the NTSB blamed hatch covers that let the hold flood. The lake carriers believed she had struck a shoal near Caribou Island. | W |
| 11 | The day after, the Mariners' Church of Detroit rang its bell 29 times, once for each man. In 1976 Gordon Lightfoot's song carried the story around the world. | W, S |
| 12 | Her bell was raised on July 4, 1995, and is kept at the Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum at Whitefish Point. A new bell, engraved with the names of the crew, rests on the wreck in its place. | W, M |

Then the crew, one name and one toll of the bell each, captain first and then
by department (the sources list them alphabetically), and two closing lines:
'WE ARE HOLDING OUR OWN.' (McSorley's last message, card 8) and TO THE
TWENTY-NINE. (Jason's choice, 2026-10-07).

Notes
- Launch day: W says June 7, 1958; M says June 8. Card 1 says "June 1958".
- Distance: W gives "about 17 miles from Whitefish Bay" (as the 11-10 On This
  Day entry does); M words it "17 miles north-northwest of Whitefish Point".
- Times are Eastern, as the sources give them. 7:10 p.m. Eastern = 6:10 p.m.
  Central, the time the screen runs by itself each 10 November.
- Left out, though sourced (W): the two 30-to-35-foot seas the Anderson's
  captain reported, and the rule changes that followed (depth finders,
  survival suits).
- "Tons" in card 4 are long tons (26,116 long tons, W and M).
